# Frame-level possession estimation

## Approach and assets

The algorithm combines local tracking evidence with match context and decodes each half as a sequence of home possession, away possession, or dead ball. The sequence model discourages short, unsupported changes, which would split otherwise coherent possessions in the ranking metric.

- **Tactical automatic tracking.** The metadata maps track slots to teams. Each row supplies ball and player positions when available. Coordinates are scaled to meters and the one-based tracking ID is shifted to the video's zero-based frame index. We compute the distance from the ball to each team's closest player and the fraction of tracked players moving at least 2 m/s when enough speeds are available. We reduce control evidence for fast balls and for duels where both teams are almost equally close.
- **Tactical Smart Tagging.** Automatically generated attack phases supply weak evidence for the tagged team, and selected defensive phases supply weak evidence for the opposition. Conflicting tags reduce the net team signal. Set pieces provide candidate restart windows. The algorithm looks for ball motion beginning near a plausible location and uses the tagged side when known. The published half ranges define where the decoder runs.
- **Asset choices.** The implementation uses the tactical timeline throughout. It requires neither video decoding nor alignment to the broadcast recording. Homography is unnecessary for the distances we compute from pitch coordinates.

The predictor uses fixed heuristics expressed in meters, speeds, and time, plus one small classifier from team shape to the team on the ball. That classifier is fitted at run time on this match's own frames, labelled by the predictor's own ball-contact evidence (algorithmic pseudo-labels, which RULES.md permits). It uses no human-labelled training data, no external match data, no ground truth and no manual annotations. Synthetic scenarios test behavior. The organizer's public sample was used once the predictor was fixed, as a pipeline check.

## Possession rules

Evidence is aggregated in five-frame blocks, about 0.2 seconds, and decoded using Viterbi dynamic programming. The best sequence balances tracking and tagging evidence against the cost of changing state.

- **Live play and handovers.** Close, sustained contact with a clear advantage over an opponent supports control. A direct handover requires contact on the pitch or stronger tagging evidence for the receiving team at that point. Equal tag evidence can support live play while the previous team retains possession. A brief touch contributes little compared with the cost of a team switch.
- **Stoppages and restarts.** Tracking outside the pitch, sustained stationary play, low player activity, and proximity to an automatic restart support dead ball. A field-wide cue covers stoppages that have no set-piece tag, such as most fouls: when, averaged over 3 s, at most about 1 in 20 tracked players moves at 2 m/s or more (fading out by 1 in 7) and the ball is not in motion, dead ball gains evidence and a player standing over the ball counts less as control. It needs no ball position, which is often missing during stoppages. The share of players running is strongly bimodal over the match, with a separate mode near zero, so the cue is not sensitive to the exact thresholds: changing each of its constants by ±40% keeps the predicted dead-ball share between 35% and 41% and the stoppage count between 102 and 117. Ball absence alone provides little evidence. For a tagged start of half, the estimated kickoff supports a dead-ball lead-in. A restart makes it easier for the decoder to return to live play, using the likely restarting side and the available tracking evidence.
- **Possession while the ball is hidden: team shape.** Most of the missing ball comes from long gaps, averaging about 21 s in the first half, where the decoder previously could only keep the last team. Every frame records each team's centroid, spread, length and width. The half's attack direction comes from where each goalkeeper stands (the team whose rearmost player sits nearer x = 0 defends that end), and it flips at half-time as it should. A small L2-regularised logistic regression from five home-minus-away shape features to "home has the ball" is fitted on this match's own blocks where the ball is visible and one team clearly controls it. These are algorithmic pseudo-labels: no human or ground-truth labels are involved. Fitted on one half and tested on the other, it predicts the controlling team on 81–83% of such blocks, and on 85–92% of the confident ones it actually uses. It is applied only to blocks with no ball contact and normal player activity. Its intercept is dropped, so a base rate cannot carry possession through a long gap on its own, and only outputs with p > 0.82 count, at a low per-block weight because neighbouring blocks are strongly correlated. It can also justify a handover during a gap. It is active on 40% of no-contact blocks and relabels about 1.3% of graded frames.
- **Interceptions.** A pass or clearance is a clear touch by one team followed, 0.12–3 s and at least 5 m later, by a clear touch by the other. If the receiver then keeps the ball for at least 6 of the next 15 frames, possession changes at the reception, without waiting for a second of sustained control. Shorter exchanges are duels, which the contact evidence already handles. The detector finds 123 interceptions over the match, split evenly between the teams.
- **Ball carrier.** When the same tracked player stays within 1.5 m of the ball for at least 0.5 s, moving with it (velocities within 2 m/s over ±2 frames), that player is carrying the ball, the challenge's own proxy for controlled possession, so that team's contact evidence is raised to full strength. A tracker identity switch ends the run. Carriers cover 20% of frames with a tracked ball, in 512 runs with a median of 1.1 s. The motivating case, a crowd where an equally close opponent cancels the proximity signal, turned out to be rare: in 96% of carrier frames, proximity already named the same team, agreeing 100% of the time. So the effect is mainly firmer evidence for sustained control, which lets a real handover pay the switch cost sooner. Damping the opponent's evidence during a carry was tried and had no effect on the output, so it was removed. Changing each carrier constant by about ±40% keeps the possession count between 208 and 219.
- **Ball out of play (Laws 15–17).** After a first decoding pass, every stoppage that begins with the ball tracked outside the pitch is traced back up to 3 s to the last clear contact on the pitch. The other team receives restart evidence for 1.2 s after play resumes, and the half is decoded again. This gives a side to throw-ins, which Smart Tagging always tags `N/A`, and to restarts with no tag at all. It fired on 34 restarts; in 27 it agreed with the side the first pass had already chosen and in 7 it changed it.
- **Free kicks.** A stoppage of at least 2 s that did not begin with the ball going out, and is not near a goal or kickoff, is treated as a probable foul. Most fouls are committed by the team without the ball, so the team in possession before the stoppage gets restart evidence, weaker than the last-touch rule's because offsides go the other way. It fires on 65 stoppages but changes the restarting team in only 2: in nearly every case the decoder had already picked that team.
- **Goals (Law 8).** From the end of a `GOALS` tag to the matching `Kick Off (After Goal)` restart, within 180 s, dead ball receives the same strong evidence as the lead-in to a half's opening kickoff. All four after-goal kickoffs in the tagging have no team, so the side that conceded is used as the kicking-off team.
- **Output.** Decoding runs in 0.2 s blocks, so each change of state is then re-placed at frame precision within one block either side. Per-frame evidence for the two states involved decides: control contact for a team, a moving ball for live play, the ball outside the pitch for dead ball. Only boundaries between segments of at least two blocks move, and ties keep the decoded position. This relabelled 364 frames across 282 boundaries. Frames outside the published half ranges receive `X`. A stoppage receives `D` regardless of the restarting side.

**Tried and dropped: filling short ball gaps.** We implemented straight-line interpolation of the ball across gaps of up to 1 s, to restore proximity evidence for handovers. It filled 0.1% of missing-ball frames: the provided tracking already bridges short gaps, and in the first half 59 gaps of 5 s or more, averaging about 21 s, account for almost all of the missing ball. The few short gaps imply ball speeds near 40 m/s, which means re-detection jumps, not real flights. The code was removed.

## Limitations

The model can miss a whistle or foul that leaves the ball moving, a restart absent from the automatic tags, or a controlled one-touch pass. A long occlusion can hide an actual handover. Noisy ball positions near the touchline can produce false stoppages. The last-touch rule needs the ball tracked near a player shortly before it goes out, so it is silent for most restarts. The free-kick rule assumes the team in possession was fouled, which is wrong for offsides and for fouls by the attacking side. Live possessions average about 18 s over the full match (median about 12 s), against roughly 10–15 s typical in top-flight football, so some open-play turnovers are probably still missed. The interception rule is the main reason that figure fell from 22.5 s, and it is moderately sensitive to its weight: ±40% moves the possession count between 184 and 221. The shape classifier is trained on frames with a visible ball and applied to frames without one, which may differ systematically (crowded boxes, aerial balls). A ball detector run on the tactical video, and calibration of the constants on other matches' open data, would address the remaining gaps.

## Verification

The organizer's validator accepted the full-match CSV. It has 199,319 rows from frame 0 through 199,318. Two independent runs on the tactical data generated byte-identical CSVs. The field-wide stoppage cue was designed and checked with label-free diagnostics only: the predicted dead-ball share (20.6% before, 39.6% after, against roughly 40–45% of time out of play in a typical LaLiga match), the number of stoppages (73 before, 113 after, against roughly 100–130 restarts in a typical match), and the share of automatic set-piece tags with a predicted stoppage within 5 s before them (82% before, 96% after). On the public five-minute pipeline check, run once after each change was fixed, the official scorer reported 0.4690 spellF1 and 0.7303 macroF1 after the stoppage cue (previously 0.4414 and 0.6941), 0.4714 and 0.7314 after the restart, goal and boundary rules, 0.4593 and 0.7360 after the shape, interception and free-kick rules, and 0.4276 and 0.7468 after the ball-carrier rule. It counted 12 predicted possessions across 17 reference possessions in that window. These gaps are within the window's bootstrap noise (about ±0.40) and were not used to choose any setting. They do show a pattern worth stating: each of the last two rule sets raised macroF1 and lowered spellF1 on this window. With 17 possessions that may be noise, but it is also what too many short false possessions would look like, and it should be checked on other matches with labels. Over the full match, the rules after the boundary refinement moved the label-free indicators towards a typical match: 216 possessions instead of 171, a median possession of 11.8 s instead of 17.2 s, with the dead-ball share (39.0%) and stoppage count (110) essentially unchanged. The organizer scores the full match against private labels.

## Reproduction

Use Python 3.13.9 with the three tactical files listed in [README.md](README.md). There are no third-party dependencies or model weights. Run:

```sh
python src/main.py --data-dir /path/to/match-bundle --output output/TEAM_NAME.csv
```

Check the generated CSV with the organizer's `evaluation/validate_submission.py`.
